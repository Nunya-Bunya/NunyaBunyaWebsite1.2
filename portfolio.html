Stage 01 · Find

A list that
refills itself.

The bottleneck in outreach is never the sending. It is running out of people worth sending to.

So the list tops itself up on a schedule. It pulls real businesses, scores each one on how much it would benefit from the thing you sell, and only keeps the ones with a contact address that actually resolves.

  • Addresses are found on the business's own site, never guessed or pattern-matched
  • Every address is checked against live mail records before it enters the list
  • Anyone already contacted is filtered out automatically
  • Bounced addresses are permanently excluded

Stage 02 · Write

Written one
at a time.

Every email is drafted against your offer, your audience and your voice, including the words you never want used. It should read like you wrote it in ten minutes, because that is the target.

The rule that does not bend: it never pretends to have studied their website. Cold outreach that fakes homework collapses the moment somebody replies and asks about it. Saying plainly that it is cold is more honest, and in an inbox full of fake compliments it stands out.

From: you@yourbusiness.com.au
Subject: websites and marketing systems for fencing contractors
Hey, I'm Ben. I build websites and marketing systems for small operators, and I'm reaching out to a batch of fencing contractors this week to see if it's useful. I handle the site, the follow-up, and the systems that turn a lead into a booked job, all done remotely so you can stay focused on the actual work. Here's some of what I've built: nunyabunya.com If you want a quick chat about what this could look like, happy to jump on a call. If the timing's wrong, no worries at all. Ben
Stage 03 · Check

Nothing leaves
unchecked.

An automated system that can write is a system that can embarrass you. So every email is tested against a rules list immediately before sending, and anything that fails is dropped rather than queued.

  • Claiming to have reviewed their site when nobody did
  • Inventing a statistic, a result, or a timeline
  • Naming a price that was never agreed
  • Breaking the voice rules the brand runs on

The check runs a second time at send, because copy can be edited after it is drafted.

Stage 04 · Send

Volume that
survives.

Anyone can send five hundred emails tomorrow. The people who do it get their domain filtered, and then their invoices and booking confirmations stop arriving too.

So sending starts low and climbs on a schedule while the domain builds a reputation. There is a hard daily ceiling that holds even if something upstream misbehaves, and a permanent record of every address contacted so nobody is emailed twice.

Stage 05 · Book

From reply
to proposal.

A reply is only useful if it turns into a conversation. Replies route to a booking link tied to your real calendar, so every slot offered is a slot that exists.

After the call, the recording is transcribed and turned into a first-draft proposal. Every quote in it is checked word for word against the transcript, and anything that cannot be verified is dropped rather than paraphrased.
